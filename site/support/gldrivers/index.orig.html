Introduction

This is the OpenGL drivers section on the Loki Support pages. On these pages you will find references, hints, and FAQs based on feedback and support request we get from our customers. Linux 3D graphics in general, and hardware accelerated graphics integrated with XFree86 in particular, are moving very fast. We expect things to settle down a bit by the end of 2000, following the adoption of XFree86 v4 into major distributions. In the meantime, we hope that the information provided here will give both the casual Linux user and the adventurous some help in getting the most out of their system. Right now, we are lacking a bit on the information for the casual user, but we hope to have step by step instructions up soon.

You will find some introductory information on drivers for the following vendors' cards below:

  • 3dfx Voodoo
  • Matrox G200/G400
  • NVIDIA TNT/TNT2/GeForce
  • Others

Please recognize that OpenGL in general, and GLX and the X server integration of GLX, are Linux system components, and are supposed to be part of the distribution you use. We can't take responsibility for installations and changes that include kernel modules, device drivers, and other sensitive parts of your Linux. We will do our best to provide you all information necessary to make educated decisions if you do not want to wait for your distribution to provide the necessary support, and, whenever possible, will respond to problems that come to our attention on these pages, but ultimately, OpenGL support will have to be a stable part of your Debian, RedHat, SuSE, and Caldera installation.

3dfx

Currently, there are two different driver families available for 3dfx cards. We suggest that you visit the drivers section on http://linux.3dfx.com for news and latest driver releases.

Mesa/FX and Glide2

The tried and true way to use 3dfx cards under Linux is using the binary releases of Glide2, combined with the Mesa/FX support in the Mesa implementation of the OpenGL API. Please refer to http://linux.3dfx.com for more information, and take a look at our step by step guide.

XFree86 4.0, DRI and Glide3

On the 3dfx site listed above you will also find information about accelerated OpenGL using XFree86 4 with the DRI for direct rendering, and a revised Glide3 library. We do not recommend using this unless you are adventurous. Firstly, applications and libraries compiled against Glide2 will not work with Glide3. Secondly, XFree86 version 4 differs significantly from XFree86 3 releases, and upgrading might affect a lot more X applications than just your games.

Matrox

The Utah-GLX implementation of the GLX protocol is available at http://utah-glx.sourceforge.net. It supports Matrox G200 and G400, based on the register specifications Matrox provided. This GLX requires use of the 3.3.6 version of the XFree86 SVGA server, earlier versions do not offer all features and bug fixes required. It is recommended to build Utah GLX with the Mesa 3.2 stable sources. See our documentation on how to obtain Mesa.

NVIDIA

NVIDIA has released a binary-only OpenGL driver for XFree86 4.0. It is available from http://www.nvidia.com/Products/Drivers.nsf. Follow the links to the latest Linux drivers and instructions for installing them.

There is also an NVIDIA driver available from the Utah-GLX project at http://utah-glx.sourceforge.net. It is based on the obfuscated source code released by NVIDIA. It offers lower performance compared to the binary-only driver released by NVIDIA themselves.

Other Vendors

There are efforts under way for a number of other cards. For instance, the Utah-GLX project offers support for the ATI Rage Pro card at present. We will update this page each time a working combination of hardware and driver comes to our attention.
